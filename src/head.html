Soft Magic

A thousand years. A hundred turns. One sword, and its will is yours.

Four peoples share a river-cut land, and three crowns have divided it between them: dwarves under the northern mountains, high elves on the old-growth coast, humans in a great eastern city, and a valley in between where humans and wood elves keep the peace together. Left alone, the world will run itself, as it does. Halfway through the age a Dark Lord rises in the southern swamp barrows and turns the orc tribes into a Host that wants every capital.

You are not a hero. You are the quiet magic inside a sword that was pulled out of the river mud in the Vale. You cannot swing yourself. You can only want things, and make the hand that holds you want them too. Everyone in this world wants something — a crown, a fortune, a fight worth a song, the winning side, the sword itself — and where they go follows from it.

The will is the gameA sword that wants nothing changes nothing. Big, deliberate desires — four points of Valor when the Dark Lord rides out, three of Counsel when a crown is near its mistake, a loud Call when the wrong hand holds you — are how the age is won. Small adjustments are how it is lost.
The Bearer cardMost of the game lives in one tab. Each decade it shows whoever holds the sword: where they are, what they want, what is coming for them, and two or three things the sword can do about it. Click an act to add a point of will to that desire (click again for more; a full desire resets). Then step the decade.
WillEvery choice spends the sword's will across six desires. Keen wins the fight; Valor goes looking for it, and for the Dark Lord when he rides out; Ward keeps the bearer alive; Glory sends them home; Call passes the blade on; Counsel is heeded at court in proportion to the bearer's renown.
Changing handsRaise Call and choose whom the sword seeks. Its bearer will travel toward them and give it up when they meet.
Land and hungerEvery crown needs more land each century: dwarves delve the mountain (and goblins come up out of the delvings), elves plant groves, humans build engine-farms that eat the forests. When a kingdom runs short it sends envoys to a neighbour, or a host.
The three mistakesEach kingdom can ruin itself once. Counsel at a capital keeps a crown steady and sways treaties. Watch the omens.
QuestsThe age has a shape: some twenty quests, from the first delvings to the last march on the fortress. Heroes take them up; the Quests tab shows who is doing what, and why.
Hard modeNo one is chosen. Each people names a champion — the best sword in the hall — who ages, sickens and dies like anyone, and is replaced by whoever is left. Prophecy does not protect them; only the sword can.
The right hourThe Dark Lord can only be killed by the sword, in a fight the sword is in. Be there.
Reading the worldPause any time. Hover the tiny markers, click tiles and people, and read the chronicle.

How to play

You are the magic inside a sword

Not a hero, not a king: the enchantment in a legendary blade. You cannot walk or fight. You can only change what the sword is — sharper, more sensing, hungrier for victory, homesick, seeking a new hand, or charismatic — and the people who carry it change with it.
Each decade, alter the sword's magical properties on the Bearer card, then step the turn and watch the age play out. Big, deliberate changes win ages; small ones change nothing.
Follow whoever holds the sword. Call it to a hero, hunt the Dark Lord when he rides out, sit a charismatic holder beside a crown that is about to make its great mistake.
